LABOUR AGREEMENTS

Agreement-based sponsorship.

An approved agreement may support 482, 186 or 494. Which programs and terms apply depends on that agreement.

This is not a separate visa or an automatic concession. 186 Labour Agreement is distinct from DE and TRT; 494 is a regional provisional visa. Review the employer’s agreement and the applicant’s conditions together.

EMPLOYER QUESTIONS

Questions before sponsoring someone.

Start with the business process, then check applicant requirements.

Sponsorship, nomination and visa: what is the difference?

For a standard 482 application, sponsorship concerns the employer’s approval to sponsor. Nomination concerns the proposed job, duties, salary and nominated worker. The visa application assesses the person’s eligibility to live and work in Australia.

186 also requires an employer nomination and a separate visa application, but does not use the same standard business sponsorship approval step as 482. A successful nomination is not a visa grant; the two applications have separate requirements.

Home Affairs: sponsoring a worker

What will the employer need to prepare?

Start with the business structure and operations, why the role is needed, actual duties, work location, proposed contract and salary. Depending on the pathway and business circumstances, supporting records may include financial statements, organisation charts, payroll information and evidence supporting the market salary.

We also check sponsorship status, recruitment or labour market testing evidence where required, and the relevant nomination criteria. The checklist is tailored to the case; an initial conversation only needs an overview, not confidential employee records.

Home Affairs: nominating a position

Can we start before selecting a candidate?

Yes. Share the business, proposed roles and timetable first. We can review recruitment and sponsorship considerations before a candidate is identified.
