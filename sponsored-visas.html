SKILLS IN DEMAND · SUBCLASS 482

482 · Skills in Demand

The Skills in Demand visa allows an approved employer to sponsor a suitably skilled professional for a nominated role. It is a temporary work visa, and the employer, position and applicant’s background are assessed as part of the process.

What the assessment considers

  • The employer’s business, sponsorship status and obligations.
  • Whether the nominated role and its terms align with the applicable requirements.
  • The applicant’s relevant skills, qualifications, work history and supporting evidence.

How we can support

We can help coordinate the employer and professional sides of the matter—from role and candidate context through pathway assessment, information gathering and application preparation. A 482 matter may also sit within a longer-term plan, which should be considered against the individual facts and current rules.

EMPLOYER NOMINATION SCHEME · SUBCLASS 186

186 · Employer Nomination Scheme

The Employer Nomination Scheme is a permanent visa pathway involving an Australian employer’s nomination. The relevant stream and requirements depend on the applicant’s background, the role and the employer’s circumstances.

Two commonly discussed streams

  • Direct Entry may be relevant for a professional seeking a permanent employer-nominated pathway without relying on a prior 482-to-186 transition. Specific criteria and exemptions need individual review.
  • Temporary Residence Transition is a later permanent residence pathway for eligible 482 or former 457 primary visa holders, including qualifying bridging visa situations. It requires qualifying sponsored employment; it is not a starting route without that background.

How we can support

We can help examine the nomination context, role, professional history and available evidence, and coordinate the preparation across employer and applicant. Where a matter has complications or a less straightforward history, we can assess the issues before mapping possible next steps.

Visa names, criteria and processes can change. The summaries above are general information, not individual migration advice. Check the current Department of Home Affairs information or speak with a qualified professional about your circumstances.

PERSONAL PREPARATION

Connect your experience with the role.

Begin with an overview, not a collection of sensitive documents.

Your background and records

Occupation, qualifications, dated work history, skills-assessment or licensing information where relevant, English evidence and current visa circumstances.

Your employer and plans

If you have an employer, outline the role, location and proposed timing. If not, explain the type of work and locations you are considering so the discussion starts from realistic options.

APPLICANT QUESTIONS

Questions about your sponsored pathway.

Check your circumstances before choosing a pathway.

Why assess the role if the employer is willing to sponsor?

The proposed occupation, actual duties and employment terms matter alongside your background. We review them together so the preparation is based on the real role, not its title alone.

What are the main 482 Core Skills requirements?

This stream generally requires a nominated occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List, suitable skills and at least one year of relevant work experience. , health and character requirements apply, with exemptions where available. Some applicants also need a skills assessment.

English scores at a glance — primary applicant

IELTS
At least 5.0 in each of listening, reading, writing and speaking (Academic or General Training).
PTE Academic
Tests taken on or after 13 September 2025: listening 33, reading 36, writing 29 and speaking 24, each a minimum.

For earlier tests, IELTS requires overall 5.0 and each skill 5.0; PTE requires overall 36 and each skill 36. The test must normally be taken within three years before applying. Exemptions may apply; Labour Agreement requirements depend on the agreement.

The salary must satisfy the applicable market salary and income-threshold rules. We check the occupation, duties, work history and evidence together. Specialist Skills and Labour Agreement streams have different rules; these Core Skills criteria are not a checklist for every 482 application.

Can I apply for 186 Direct Entry without first holding 482?

Yes, if you meet the Direct Entry requirements. Applicants are generally under 45, have an eligible occupation, at least three years of relevant work experience, a suitable skills assessment before applying and . Age, skills-assessment and experience exemptions may apply in specific circumstances.

English scores at a glance — primary applicant

IELTS
At least 6.0 in each of listening, reading, writing and speaking (Academic or General Training).
PTE Academic
Tests taken on or after 7 August 2025: listening 47, reading 48, writing 51 and speaking 54, each a minimum.

For PTE tests taken on or before 6 August 2025, each skill must be at least 50, subject to the applicable validity rules. Test results must normally be current within the three-year application window. Eligible citizenship and passport evidence can also establish Competent English; other approved tests are available.

Home Affairs: Competent English scores and accepted evidence

An eligible employer nomination, any required occupational registration, and health and character requirements also matter. We assess your evidence and the employer’s proposed role before recommending this permanent residence pathway.

Can 186 TRT be my first employer-sponsored application?

Not without the required temporary sponsorship background. You must hold 482 or former 457 as the primary applicant, or a qualifying bridging visa linked to that visa history. TRT generally requires at least two years of eligible full-time sponsored employment in Australia in the three years before applying, plus an employer nomination. Applicants are generally under 45 and need ; specified age exemptions may apply. Other criteria still apply.

Does holding 482 mean I will automatically obtain 186?

No. A later permanent-residence pathway needs a separate assessment of the relevant stream, nomination and applicant requirements.
