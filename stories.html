BUSINESS CASES

Roles, nominations and workplace preparation shaped around the business.

FINTECH · VIC

Fintech: Software Engineer nomination (186 DE)

186 · Direct Entry nomination

The brief

A digital payments business needed a new Software Engineer role for product development and operations.

The key question

The preparation needed to explain why this new role was necessary, what the engineer would do and how the proposed salary was supported.

How we approached it

We mapped the engineering duties to the business’s actual projects, then brought together the financial, employment and company records. For salary evidence, we used independent market information and more than 20 relevant job advertisements.

Why these records mattered

The business case, duties and salary evidence supported the same explanation. The nomination did not rely on a job title or a standard position description alone.

Project outcome

Nomination approved without a further information request.

This is an employer nomination outcome, not a visa grant.

FINANCIAL SERVICES · VIC

Financial services: Technology lead nomination (186 DE)

186 · Direct Entry nomination

The brief

A financial services business was building internal IT capability for its digital transformation.

The key question

The proposed leadership role needed a clear technical purpose within the business, rather than a broad management description.

How we approached it

We connected the role with platform integration, cybersecurity and the employer’s operating plans. Market salary research, employment classification review and labour market testing records were organised around that role.

Why these records mattered

That preparation made the link between the technical responsibilities and the employer’s plans explicit. Each part of the nomination could be read against the same role.

Project outcome

Nomination approved without a further information request.

This is an employer nomination outcome, not a visa grant.

HEALTHCARE · QLD

Healthcare: recruitment across several sites

HR & workforce support

The brief

A healthcare and training business needed recruitment, onboarding and employment records to work across several locations.

The key question

The work had to connect local recruitment activity with records that could also support future sponsorship matters.

How we approached it

We brought role classifications, advertising templates and onboarding records into a shared framework. Centralised tracking and reporting linked the locations, with preparation for potential 482 and 186 matters kept in view.

Why these records mattered

Reusable records reduced the need to assemble each recruitment history from scratch when a later staffing or sponsorship question arose.

Project outcome

Shared HR processes and reusable recruitment records, with centralised tracking and reporting.

CROSS-BORDER BUSINESS · NSW

Cross-border business: Australian hiring and payroll

HR systems & integration

The brief

An overseas business needed a consistent approach to hiring, payroll and staff coordination in its Australian operations.

The key question

The Australian records and responsibilities needed to be clear to both local staff and overseas management.

How we approached it

We connected recruitment, onboarding and payroll records with the staffing plan, clarified communication with overseas management and prepared templates for later sponsorship and 482 nominations.

Why these records mattered

The framework addressed the Australian operations, rather than simply carrying over the overseas group’s processes.

Project outcome

A unified framework for HR, recruitment and payroll coordination across the Australian operations.

INDIVIDUAL CASES

Connecting an applicant’s qualifications and experience with the proposed work.

RESOURCES · WA

Resources: Materials Engineer preparation (186 DE)

186 · Direct Entry

The brief

A Materials Engineer in Western Australia’s resources sector was preparing for an employer-nominated role.

The key question

The technical experience, nominated duties and skills assessment preparation needed to be read together—not as unrelated files.

How we approached it

We reviewed the connection between the applicant’s technical background and the proposed duties, alongside the employer information. Skills assessment requirements and sponsorship compliance were considered with those records.

Why these records mattered

This kept the applicant’s experience and the employer’s need in the same account of the proposed work, rather than relying on the occupation title alone.

Support focus

Employer nomination, skills assessment preparation and the connection between technical experience and the proposed role.

CONSTRUCTION · WA

Construction: Civil Engineering Draftsperson (482)

482 · Skills in Demand

The brief

A Civil Engineering Draftsperson case in Western Australia required a review of the proposed work against the occupation criteria.

The key question

The role description needed to explain the actual drafting duties, not stop at the position’s name.

How we approached it

We reviewed the work behind the title, organised the duties and identified the records that supported them. The employer sponsorship preparation connected this role information with the occupation criteria.

FINANCIAL SERVICES · VIC

Financial services: Investment Financial Adviser (482)

482 · Skills in Demand

The brief

An Investment Financial Adviser in Victoria needed their qualifications and professional experience related to the proposed position.

The key question

Qualifications alone would not explain the fit; the preparation needed to show their relevance to the work.

How we approached it

We reviewed the qualifications, refined the role information and organised the case around the applicant’s education and professional experience. Each part was presented in relation to the proposed duties.

Why these records mattered

The records could then be followed as one professional background, rather than separate lists of certificates, jobs and duties.

Support focus

Qualifications review, role refinement and a coherent presentation of the applicant’s professional background.
