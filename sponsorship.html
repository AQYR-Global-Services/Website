SKILLS IN DEMAND · SUBCLASS 482

482 · Skills in Demand

The Skills in Demand visa allows an approved employer to sponsor a suitably skilled professional for a nominated role. It is a temporary work visa, and the employer, position and applicant’s background are assessed as part of the process.

What the assessment considers

  • The employer’s business, sponsorship status and obligations.
  • Whether the nominated role and its terms align with the applicable requirements.
  • The applicant’s relevant skills, qualifications, work history and supporting evidence.

How we can support

We can help coordinate the employer and professional sides of the matter—from role and candidate context through pathway assessment, information gathering and application preparation. A 482 matter may also sit within a longer-term plan, which should be considered against the individual facts and current rules.

EMPLOYER NOMINATION SCHEME · SUBCLASS 186

186 · Employer Nomination Scheme

The Employer Nomination Scheme is a permanent visa pathway involving an Australian employer’s nomination. The relevant stream and requirements depend on the applicant’s background, the role and the employer’s circumstances.

Two commonly discussed streams

  • Direct Entry may be relevant for a professional seeking a permanent employer-nominated pathway without relying on a prior 482-to-186 transition. Specific criteria and exemptions need individual review.
  • Temporary Residence Transition is a later permanent residence pathway for eligible 482 or former 457 primary visa holders, including qualifying bridging visa situations. It requires qualifying sponsored employment; it is not a starting route without that background.

How we can support

We can help examine the nomination context, role, professional history and available evidence, and coordinate the preparation across employer and applicant. Where a matter has complications or a less straightforward history, we can assess the issues before mapping possible next steps.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Questions about sponsored work.

Sponsorship, nomination and visa: what is the difference?

For a standard 482 application, sponsorship concerns the employer’s approval to sponsor. Nomination concerns the proposed job, duties, salary and nominated worker. The visa application assesses the person’s eligibility to live and work in Australia.

186 also requires an employer nomination and a separate visa application, but does not use the same standard business sponsorship approval step as 482. A successful nomination is not a visa grant; the two applications have separate requirements.

What are the main 482 Core Skills requirements?

This stream generally requires a nominated occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List, suitable skills and at least one year of relevant work experience. , health and character requirements apply, with exemptions where available. Some applicants also need a skills assessment.

Can I apply for 186 Direct Entry without first holding 482?

Yes, if you meet the Direct Entry requirements. Applicants are generally under 45, have an eligible occupation, at least three years of relevant work experience, a suitable skills assessment before applying and . Age, skills-assessment and experience exemptions may apply in specific circumstances.

Can 186 TRT be my first employer-sponsored application?

Not without the required temporary sponsorship background. You must hold 482 or former 457 as the primary applicant, or a qualifying bridging visa linked to that visa history. TRT generally requires at least two years of eligible full-time sponsored employment in Australia in the three years before applying, plus an employer nomination. What will the employer need to prepare?

Start with the business structure and operations, why the role is needed, actual duties, work location, proposed contract and salary. Depending on the pathway and business circumstances, supporting records may include financial statements, organisation charts, payroll information and evidence supporting the market salary.

We also check sponsorship status, recruitment or labour market testing evidence where required, and the relevant nomination criteria. The checklist is tailored to the case; an initial conversation only needs an overview, not confidential employee records.

Home Affairs: nominating a position

I already have an employer. Why review the role?

An employer’s willingness to sponsor is the starting point. The nominated occupation, actual duties and employment terms also need to meet the relevant requirements. We review these alongside your background to identify preparation gaps early.

Is 482 the same as permanent residence?

No. The Skills in Demand visa is a temporary work visa. Subclass 186 is a permanent employer-nominated visa; any later application needs its own assessment.

Does holding 482 automatically lead to 186?

No. A permanent visa is a separate application. The relevant stream, nomination and applicant requirements need to be met.
